Nutrient removal — nitrogen

Nitrogen assimilated by this bed

Eger et al. 2023 (Nat Commun 14:1894) publish 124 g N/m²/yr for Laminaria/Saccharina — a genus-specific MASS, from net primary production × literature tissue composition. Applied to the locked  ha bed.

Value, as a band

NOT a benefit transfer. Eger et al. publish no genus-specific nitrogen-only euro, which is why this row was omitted before. This is our own transparent replacement-cost synthesis: their mass × an external European nitrogen price, each half checkable on its own. Floor €1.5/kg N — SHARON/ANAMMOX side-stream treatment, the cheapest place in the system to strip nitrogen (US EPA 832-F-07-017 p.5, citing STOWA 2006). Ceiling €5–20/kg N — societal damage from N-runoff (European Nitrogen Assessment ch.22, Brink & van Grinsven, CUP 2011, p.513). The two ends answer different questions and are 3–13× apart, so the band is the result; a mean of it would be a fabrication. Neither price is inflation-indexed.

The weakness, stated first: replacement cost needs the man-made alternative to be a least-cost substitute (Shabman & Batie condition 2). A treatment plant removes nitrogen permanently as N₂ gas; kelp assimilates it into tissue that is largely exported as detritus and substantially remineralised. The services are not equivalent, so this is an upper bound on an upper bound. Demonstrated demand (condition 3) is not claimed. Overestimation warning: Barbier 2016.

Cross-check: Eger et al.'s own global nitrogen figure pairs $73,831/ha/yr with 620 kg N/ha/yr — an implicit $/kg N, about × the engineering floor and × the societal ceiling. Transferring their dollar figure would import a price no European source supports.

Four limitations that travel with this statement

    Transfer test — a different site, in France

    Le Conquet & the Molène archipelago, Iroise Sea, Brittany

    The engine has to transfer to other Atlantic locations, so it was run end to end somewhere else. Every figure in this box is French and none of it is an Irish euro. The coastal-protection chain stopped at all 53 Irish sites for one physical reason: the only published L. hyperborea attenuation coefficients (Dubi & Tørum 1996) were measured at 4–10 m, and the Irish beds sit at 0.8 m or 12–17 m. This bed's core sits inside that band.

    Coefficients inside their tested range

    First time anywhere in this project. The Sherkin control re-derived 7.9%/15.2% before any French data was touched; the site's own wave bearing (° , CMEMS IBI 2015–2024) was measured, never carried from Ireland, and agrees with an independent NWSHELF model to °.

    Avoided damage (primary method) not computable

    The same verdict as in Ireland, and for a reason that has nothing to do with depth: no published method's inputs are produced by this pipeline. All three need a hazard value at the receptor, with and without the habitat; this pipeline produces a wave-height reduction offshore. The depth constraint was the first blocker in Ireland, not the only one.

    Replacement cost, annualised

    Illustrative upper bound (Barbier 2016), central rate, over a measured  m frontage — the shoreline that has both valid-band kelp on the wave path and a built receptor below 5 m behind it. Shabman & Batie condition 3 (demonstrated demand) is met here, where it fails at Sherkin: receptors sit below 5 m within 200 m of the IGN land–sea limit. Conditions 1 and 2 are still only partly met. The unit rate is Irish (OPW, €5,621/m) applied on a French coast — a cross-country transfer, flagged not hidden, and the Iroise is a harder coast than the Wexford site it came from. Scaling by the energy reduction is our simplification, not a published method. This is one-off capital annualised at 4% over 50 years; never read beside the annual Irish rows as if comparable.
