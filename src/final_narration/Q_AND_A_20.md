# Twenty jury questions and evidence-based answers

Build `6a84de90fe69`. Figures refer to the default Cape Clear / South Harbour case unless stated otherwise. Source identifiers and full assumptions are available in the assessment report and NUMBERS_AND_CLAIMS_REGISTER.md.

1. **Have you mapped a current kelp forest?** No. We expose historical habitat references and dated observations. The assumed calculation cell is not a confirmed current bed. A contemporary, georeferenced habitat survey is the next requirement.
2. **Why Cape Clear?** It combines inspectable historical evidence, dated observations, directional offshore waves, depth inputs, mapped assets and a documented South Harbour engineering context. It supports a conditional prototype. Copper Point, Bantry and Sherkin retain supporting roles and their own limitations.
3. **Why do the NPWS layers disagree?** The selected location is represented differently in two reference products with inherited survey lineage. We preserve the conflict and do not choose a current ground truth from it. Source reconciliation and repeat observation are needed.
4. **Are the reference sources independent?** Not necessarily. Shared or inherited historical survey lineage means agreement would not be independent validation. The report and graph explicitly preserve that caveat.
5. **What did machine learning establish?** The earlier experiment targeted intertidal macroalgae. The corrected experiment's external-control false-positive rate was 63.45%, above its 30% diagnostic threshold. This is diagnostic agreement with references, not current ecological accuracy. No classifier result is presented as a Cape Clear subtidal kelp map.
6. **What does 8.43% mean?** The deterministic model predicts that percentage reduction in wave height within an assumed cell under the default direction, depth and laboratory coefficient assumptions. It is not a shoreline or building result.
7. **What happens if depth changes?** At an additional two metres, the same default-direction scenario yields approximately 3.56% within-cell wave-height attenuation. The sensitivity remains conditional. The original depth datum needs verification and the slider is not a tide or sea-level forecast.
8. **Is the offshore wave direction the travel direction?** The source reports direction FROM. The geometry uses travel direction FROM plus 180 degrees. The default is FROM 210 degrees, travelling 30 degrees. This convention is tested.
9. **Can you prove a road is protected?** No. Candidate assets are spatial context. Nearshore propagation, shoreline hazard, exposure and damage modelling have not been completed.
10. **What would be needed to estimate avoided damage?** Verified habitat geometry and condition, consistent depth and water-level datums, calibrated wave transformation and flooding, event probabilities, asset exposure and appropriate damage functions. Benefit estimates also require a defensible counterfactual and uncertainty analysis.
11. **How is €562,090 calculated?** One hundred assumed metres multiplied by the pooled published funding benchmark, approximately €5,620.9046 per metre, multiplied by a 100% independent budget share. Full precision is retained in the engine. It is an engineering funding comparator.
12. **Does 8.43% set the euro value?** No. The funding share is an independent assumption. Changing wave attenuation alone must not change the funding formula.
13. **Is the annual equivalent an annual saving?** No. Approximately €26,165 per year annualises the pooled capital comparator at 4% over 50 years. It does not measure annual benefits and omits maintenance and habitat delivery costs.
14. **Are 109 taxa evidence of kelp biodiversity?** They are distinct taxon names in a mixed geographic inventory of 154 records. Habitat association and comparable sampling effort are absent. We cannot infer a kelp-bed biodiversity score or local abundance from this inventory.
15. **Can fisheries values be added to the engineering comparator?** No. A local habitat-specific fisheries benefit and a compatible valuation basis have not been established. Adding unrelated literature values would create an unsupported total.
16. **Can restoration start now?** This tool recommends investigation, not restoration approval. Current habitat condition, causes of degradation, substrate, feasibility and permissions must first be established.
17. **What is the AI actually doing?** A local graph links evidence, contradictions, assumptions, calculation outputs and report sections. Text retrieval and current application state provide context. Optional Gemma2:2b routing helps interpret a question. Controlled answer templates and the deterministic engine supply claims and numbers. This is GraphRAG, not fine-tuning.
18. **What if Ollama fails or gives an unsafe answer?** The browser provides an immediate deterministic answer and the main assessment remains available offline. Model output cannot author new scientific claims or numbers. Slow and unavailable-model paths and hostile questions are explicitly tested.
19. **Can it transfer to another coast or habitat?** The case and report contracts can transfer. The evidence, model domain, coefficients and validation cannot be assumed transferable. Supporting cases demonstrate that unsupported calculations stay unavailable.
20. **What is the most useful next investment?** A targeted georeferenced habitat and depth verification around the selected cell, followed by a calibrated nearshore wave model tied to actual receptors. That work would test whether the present conditional pathway can support a protection claim.
